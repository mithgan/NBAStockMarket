The one idea

You get paid for being right early — not for box scores.

In fantasy, a 30-point night is worth 30 points no matter who expected it. Here, a player's price only moves when reality beats what the market already priced in. That single difference turns a box-score game into an investing game.

⭐

Owning a superstar isn't enough

Everyone already agrees Jokić is elite — it's baked into his price. Buy him at full value and a triple-double barely moves your portfolio. No surprise, no return.

🚀

The money is in the unknown

A second-year guy the crowd slept on drops 35 and his price jumps — because he just beat expectations. If you bought last week, you win. Edge = insight nobody's priced yet.

⏳

Time is an asset class

You can own a 2029 draft pick or a 19-year-old prospect years before they pay off. Fantasy can't do that — it only knows tonight's lineup.

Why it's not fantasy

Production vs. expectations

Same league, same players — completely different game. Fantasy rewards what players do. A market rewards being right about value before the crowd.

Fantasy / DFS

Reward = raw production
  • Score = the sum of your players' box-score stats
  • You always want the best players
  • You own a locked roster for the period
  • Only active players exist
  • Horizon = this week / this season
VS

NBA Stock Exchange

Reward = beating expectations
  • Score = the change in value of your assets
  • You want the players the market is wrong about
  • You trade shares — buy or sell anyone, anytime
  • Players + draft picks + prospects + teams + futures
  • Horizon = this month → multiple seasons

How prices move

The market sets the price. The model sets the gravity.

No real-money order book is required — a market maker always provides liquidity. Price moves from two forces, and the balance between them is the single most important tuning knob in the whole product.

D

Demand force — the real market

When users (capped at $10k each, with position limits) collectively buy a player, scarce capital pushes the price up; net selling pushes it down. Fake money, but real, constrained supply & demand.

G

Gravity force — the anchor

A model-derived intrinsic value (forward-looking) pulls the price toward fundamentals over time, so it can't bubble to infinity like a memecoin. This is the "day of reckoning" a player share otherwise lacks.

new_price = old_price + demand_force  // (net buys − net sells) × liquidity_depth
              + gravity_force  // (intrinsic_value − price) × reversion_rate

intrinsic_value = slow_factors(age_curve, contract, pedigree, role)
                  + fast_factors(recent_perf, trends, news) × uncertainty

The twist that makes it investing and not DraftKings: recent performance is weighted by how uncertain a player's future still is. A rookie's 35-point game swings his intrinsic value hard; a 34-year-old's identical night barely moves it. You're pricing trajectories, not box scores.

Why this one survives

Every predecessor died one of two ways. We dodge both.

  • 💀 Real money → illegal or a Ponzi. Football Index (£90M gone), Mojo, Fantex all died on regulation or unsustainable payouts. → We never touch real money. Virtual capital, no cash-out.
  • 😴 Fake money → boring. No-stakes markets usually die of "why do I care." → Reputation is the reward. A permanent, public track record of the calls you made early.
  • 🎯 No anchor → memecoin chaos. → Model gravity ties every price to a forward-looking fundamental.
  • 🧾 Licensing → day-one shutdown. → Start with draft prospects & picks (the most fun, least rights-encumbered slice) while pursuing NBPA/data licenses.
Legal posture (concept stage, not legal advice): a free-to-play game using virtual currency with no purchase requirement and no cash-out is generally neither a security (no investment of real money / expectation of monetary profit) nor gambling (no real consideration or prize). The "stock market" framing is a game theme, not a financial product. This is the deliberate line that keeps the concept out of the gray area.
